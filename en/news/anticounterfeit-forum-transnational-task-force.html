Vlasta-Consulting Proposes Key Initiative at Anti-Counterfeit Forum

Representatives of Vlasta-Consulting played a key role in the "Anti-Counterfeit" Forum, proposing an initiative aimed at a systemic solution to the problem of cross-border trafficking of counterfeit products in the countries of the Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU).

Representatives of Vlasta-Consulting played a key role in the "Anti-Counterfeit" Forum, proposing an initiative aimed at a systemic solution to the problem of cross-border trafficking of counterfeit products in the countries of the Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU).

The company participated as a speaker in the first discussion session, "Intellectual Order. Practice of Rights Holders Protection in the EAEU." During his speech, Konstantin Shestera, Operational Director of Vlasta-Consulting, clearly demonstrated the legal gap hindering the effective fight against the illegal market.

He noted that in its practice, the company encounters a situation where a significant amount of information it gathers about the channels of movement and production of counterfeit goods cannot be promptly transferred to the competent authorities. This is due to existing legal and institutional barriers.

"We have encountered a situation where we have nowhere to take the volume of information we can obtain and are willing to share with the authorities," stated Konstantin Shestera. "In the case of the EEC [Eurasian Economic Commission], it is more of a strategic matter, and they cannot guarantee operational efficiency. And in the case of the FTS [Federal Customs Service], their ability to engage is limited in a number of instances due to the absence of import within the single market of the EAEU."

As a solution to this systemic problem, Vlasta-Consulting proposed creating an operational headquarters under the auspices of the EEC—a Transnational Operational Headquarters (TOSh). Its goal would be to unite the efforts of the customs and law enforcement agencies of the EAEU member states and the private sector to jointly develop tactical solutions.

According to the initiative's concept, the headquarters would be tasked with collecting and analyzing information from all process participants, as well as setting specific tasks for national structures to execute.

"This would make it possible to combine the efforts of both customs authorities, businesses, and the EEC," emphasized Shestera. "To formulate tasks and pass them down for execution to the very same customs services of the EAEU countries, and finally, to set this mechanism in motion, because right now it is at a standstill."

The proposed initiative received lively feedback from forum participants and was recognized as promising for further detailed elaboration with regulators.
